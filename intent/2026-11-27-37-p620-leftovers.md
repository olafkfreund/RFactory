# Intent: remove the p620 RFactory pod leftovers (after the fallback week)

## Problem

RFactory moved to the factory cluster on 2026-11-24 (#33). The p620 pod was stopped with `deploy/pod.sh down`, and its data was kept for 7 days as the fallback. That week ends on **2026-12-01**. Nothing may be removed before then, and nothing without the owner's OK.

Issue #37 lists four things to remove. Four things are unclear before anyone can act on it.

**1. "No problems" is not defined.** The issue says to clean up "if the cluster deployment has had no problems". Nobody has written down what is checked, so the go/no-go would be a guess.

**2. Two of the four items are still used.** #33 kept `deploy/pod.sh` as a development path (`README.md:15`), and the desktop verify scripts still start the local pod:

- `deploy/k3d/k14-env.sh:27-35` and `deploy/k3d/k11.sh:45` run `deploy/pod.sh up`. So does `desktop/verify/liveview.sh:39-41,116`, which recreates the pod to test that a removed member loses access.
- `deploy/pod.sh:27` refuses to start without `scope.json`, and `deploy/k3d/k14-env.sh:74,139` read it.
- The verify scripts call `http://127.0.0.1:4180` (`deploy/k3d/k14-env.sh:113`) with session cookies the owner supplies for an approver, a member, a non-member and an admin. A fresh cookie needs a Keycloak login through the `127.0.0.1:4180` callback, which `deploy/pod.sh:143` hard-codes.

So deleting `scope.json` or the Keycloak callback ends more than the fallback. It also stops fresh sign-in to the local pod and the desktop verify scripts as they are. #33 decided both things: keep `pod.sh` (decision 4) and remove the callback (decision 6). They conflict.

**3. The local web image may not rebuild.** `deploy/pod.sh:41` builds the image without setting `CHROME_VERSION`, so `web/Containerfile:7` pins Chrome `157.0.8103.78-1`. #33 recorded that this version no longer installs (plan deviation 4); CI passes an empty version instead. Today the build may only work because of cached layers. Removing the image and those layers could leave `pod.sh up` unable to build. Not tested.

**4. The issue's list does not match what is on p620.** Read-only inventory on p620, 2026-11-27:

The issue also gives the rollback in the wrong order: it starts the p620 pod first and scales the cluster to 0 after. #33's approved order is the reverse (Constraints), so that two instances never run against the same CFactory and desktop runner.

## Proposed outcome

On or after 2026-12-01, and only after the owner's OK:

- **A written go/no-go check has passed on the revision running that day**, and its result is recorded in #37. The spec defines the exact steps. The check must show all of this:
  1. **Healthy now:** the Deployment, the pod and the Argo Application are healthy. (2026-11-27: 1/1, 4/4 Ready, 0 restarts, `Synced` and `Healthy`.) This alone proves little: the probes only check a ping and open ports.
  2. **Working now, from outside:** a fresh sign-in through `https://rfactory.freundcloud.org.uk`, a brain turn (a standup makes no AI call, `README.md:31`), a call with a spoken answer, and an approved board share. These cover the tunnel, Keycloak, the shared Claude login and the CFactory keys.
  3. **Stable over the week:** no unexplained crash or failed start since the cutover. Deploys replace the pod (today's is 12 hours old), so the restart counter does not show this; the spec must name a source that does, or the owner attests it.
  4. **No open problem caused by the move.** #38 is closed. #67 (own transcription hears nothing) is open and also reproduces locally; the owner decides whether it blocks.
  5. **No record is lost:** every file in the p620 volume is in the PVC `rfactory-data` with the same SHA-256 sum, and records written in the cluster during the week are still there after a pod replacement. This is the last moment the comparison is possible.
  6. **The fallback was not needed:** the owner confirms the p620 pod was not started as a fallback during the week. A start for development or verification does not count.
- **The leftovers the owner chose are gone from p620**, and each removal is ticked in #37 with what was removed.
- **Everything still in use is untouched** (see Constraints), and the cluster instance still answers.
- **The local pod is in a known state.** Either `pod.sh up` is shown to work after the removals (it builds, starts and takes a sign-in), or the README says plainly what no longer works and a follow-up issue covers it.
- **If the check fails**, nothing is removed, the fallback stays, and #37 gets a new date.

## Affected users and systems

- **p620:** the owner's rootless podman store (volume, images), `~/.config/rfactory/`, `~/.config/rfactory-verify/` and `~/.local/share/rfactory/`.
- **Keycloak:** client `rfactory` in realm `factory`. It is shared with the cluster deployment.
- **The cluster deployment** (`k3d-factory`, namespaces `factory` and `rfactory-desktops`): read only, for the go/no-go check.
- **The local pod and the desktop verify scripts** (`deploy/pod.sh`, `deploy/k3d/k14-env.sh`, `deploy/k3d/k11.sh`, `desktop/verify/*.sh`): they depend on `scope.json`, the Claude login home, the local image and the local callback.
- **The owner:** decides go or no-go, approves every removal, and makes the Keycloak change.

## Constraints

- **Not before 2026-12-01.** Until then only read-only checks.
- **Owner approval before any removal**, given after the go/no-go result is shown. An approved intent, spec or plan is not that approval.
- **Never remove:**
  - cluster data: the PVC `rfactory-data`, the five RFactory Secrets, `factory-cli-creds`, and everything in `rfactory-desktops`;
  - `~/.config/rfactory/` itself and every file in it that the owner has not named. `deploy/k8s/bootstrap-secrets.sh:16` reads `scope.cluster.json`, `cfactory-read-key`, `cfactory-write-key`, `stt-path`, `keycloak-client-secret`, `oauth2-cookie-secret`, `roles.env` and `desktop-runner/` from it;
  - `~/.config/rfactory-verify/`;
  - the Keycloak client `rfactory` itself, its cluster callback `https://rfactory.freundcloud.org.uk/oauth2/callback` and its cluster web origin. The change removes one entry and must leave every other entry as it was;
  - shared images: anything in the registry (`ghcr.io/olafkfreund/rfactory-*`), anything in Docker or k3d, and every podman image that is not proven to be RFactory's (`pfactory`, `siphon`, Keycloak, Postgres, toolboxes and the rest);
  - every podman volume the owner has not named, including the unnamed ones;
  - the load-balancer port `127.0.0.1:18443`, which #33 kept for the verify scripts.
- **Remove by exact name or ID only**, for podman objects and for files. No `podman image prune`, `podman system prune`, `podman volume prune` or forced image removal: the store is shared with other projects.
- **Rollback until removal** is #33's, in this order:
  1. set `replicas: 0` in factory-gitops and wait for the pod to stop;
  2. run `deploy/pod.sh up` with the usual settings, from a clean, pushed checkout;
  3. repoint the hostname or callback as needed. The local pod answers only at `http://127.0.0.1:4180`.

  Records written in the cluster are not copied back.
- **Rollback after removal** is weaker, and the owner should accept that knowingly:
  - the images must be rebuilt. That needs a clean, pushed checkout, a Nix build for the STT image, and a web build that may fail on the Chrome pin (Problem 3);
  - the old records exist only in the cluster PVC. A new local volume starts empty, and is only created when writes or shares are on (`deploy/pod.sh:66,78`);
  - without the Keycloak callback, nobody can sign in to a local pod until it is added again;
  - `pod.sh up` also refuses to start without `scope.json` and the Claude login home (`deploy/pod.sh:27,37`). That login has no refresh path, so it goes stale.
- **No secrets printed**, and no secret file opened to decide anything. File names and modes are enough.
- **A bus post** before the Keycloak change, because the client is shared with the live deployment.

## Open questions

1. **Is the go/no-go check right?** Recommendation: accept the six items. Items 2 and 6 need the owner; items 1 and 5 can be read by an agent; items 3 and 4 need the owner's judgement on the evidence.
2. **Does #67 block the cleanup?** Recommendation: no. It reproduces locally, so the p620 fallback would not fix it.
3. **Keep `scope.json`?** The issue says "remove if it is no longer used". It is still used by `pod.sh` and `k14-env.sh`. Recommendation: keep it. Leave the two old copies alone as well, unless the owner looks at them and names them for removal.
4. **Delete the `127.0.0.1:4180` Keycloak callback?** #33 decided to delete it (decision 6) and to keep `pod.sh` (decision 4). After the deletion nobody can sign in to a local pod, so the verify scripts cannot get fresh cookies. Pointing them at the cluster is not enough: `liveview.sh` restarts the pod with a changed member list. Recommendation: keep the callback for now, and open a follow-up issue to decide how the verify scripts sign in. Delete it when that lands. It is a loopback address, so the exposure is small. This changes #33's decision 6 and needs the owner's explicit yes.
5. **Is `pod.sh` still a development path, or retired?** Recommendation: keep it, as #33 decided. Retiring it is a larger change (verify scripts, tests, README) and belongs in its own issue.
6. **Remove the local images if the rebuild is unproven?** Recommendation: first prove a clean rebuild of the web image, without touching the existing one. If it fails, fix `pod.sh` in a separate change before removing the image, or keep the image.
7. **Remove the leftovers the issue does not list?** Recommendation:
   - spike images: remove, the spike is finished and `spike/run.sh` rebuilds them;
   - spike profile volumes: keep unless the owner names them. A rebuild cannot restore a signed-in browser profile;
   - untagged build layers: remove only those proven to be RFactory builds and not parents of an image that stays. How to prove it is for the spec;
   - `claude-home` and the pulled `oauth2-proxy` image: keep while `pod.sh` stays.
8. **Keep a copy of the volume's two records before deleting it?** They are 16K. Recommendation: yes, one archive in a place the owner names, so the deletion never depends on the cluster PVC alone.
9. **Who runs the removals?** Recommendation: the owner's session on p620, one command at a time, after the OK. Not Codex or agy.
